# Lore — options, not a bible

Item 5. This is a fork in the road with three roads on it, not a setting
document. Pick one (or a hybrid) and the rest of the file becomes writable.

## What the build already asserts

Anything below has to fit these, because they are already on screen:

- **A long stone hall.** Candles down both walls, an apse at the far end, a
  rose window behind it, volumetric fog. It reads as a basilica. Two fighters
  spawn at the near end and walk toward the light.
- **Pious Force** — heavy layered coat, wide brim, wrapped hands. Grappler:
  plants, commits, swings slow and heavy. Damage falls out of how long his
  buttons take, so he hurts.
- **Veiled Shadow** — covered, bladed stance, fast. Striker: leads with kicks,
  spinning backfist at 0.42s.
- **They fight each other.** There is no third party in the build, no crowd,
  no announcer.

One thing the hall should probably *not* be is a working place of worship. A
duel on a consecrated floor makes the fight an act of desecration and drags the
whole game into an argument nobody asked for. Every option below assumes the
hall stopped being used for what it was built for a long time ago.

## Three premises

### A — The Vigil

The hall is a waystation on a road nobody finishes. Whoever holds it keeps the
candles lit; when they go out, the road closes. Fighters come to be *measured*.
Winning does not take the hall — it earns the right to keep sitting in it.

Pious Force keeps the lamps and has for years. Veiled Shadow is what arrives
when they burn low, and neither of them is sure whether she is a test or a
consequence. Duels are a rite, so both of them can be entirely sincere and
still swing at full force.

Tone: patient, ritual, slow-burning. Fits the candles and the fog best.

### B — The Ledger

Something was taken out of the hall, long enough ago that nobody agrees on
what. Everyone who walks in is claiming a debt. The fight is not about the
debt — it is about **method**, and that is the whole argument:

Pious Force settles by hand, in the open, and considers the matter closed the
moment it is settled. Veiled Shadow collects quietly and never announces it,
because a debt collected loudly makes a second one. Two people who agree the
debt is real and cannot agree on a single thing after that.

Tone: personal, grudging, a little funny. Best for banter and for a rematch
structure — you can run this indefinitely.

### C — The Last Room

Outside is gone. Not explained, not shown — gone. The hall is what is left and
these two are the last people who remember why it was built. They fight because
it is the only thing left that takes full attention, and a person who stops
paying attention here stops.

Tone: melancholy, quiet, weight on every hit. Hardest to write, best payoff.
Also the one that most justifies a hall with nobody else in it.

**Recommendation: B.** It is the only one of the three that generates dialogue
for free — the disagreement is stated, both sides are defensible, and you can
write a hundred barks off it without settling it. A is the best *atmosphere*
match, so a hybrid works: run B's argument inside A's ritual.

## The two, under any premise

Fixed regardless of which fork you take:

**Pious Force.** Devout, and written that way straight — his faith is where he
thinks his strength comes from, and he says so. The line that stays out of his
mouth is the clever one at the religion's expense; that is the Venator problem
and it is the only hard rule on him. He does not quote scripture either, in a
taunt or anywhere else, because holy text used as trash talk is the same move
dressed up as respect. He speaks about *himself* — what is lent to him, what he
does not own, what he will not take credit for. Already written and generated:
"I only praise Him", "Strength is lent. Never owned.", "I seek no glory. It was
never mine to keep."

**Veiled Shadow.** The opposite failure mode: she says as little as possible
and what she does say is about not being seen. Not mystical, not cursed —
professional. Her humour is dry and lands after the fight, not during it.
Already written: "You will not see the one that lands.", "You were never
fighting me."

The pairing works because neither of them is the villain and neither is a
comment on the other's belief. One is loud about where his strength comes from,
the other refuses to say. That is a real disagreement between two people who
would probably like each other.

## Open, for you

1. **Which premise** — A, B, C, or B-inside-A.
2. **Do they know each other?** First meeting reads very differently from the
   fortieth. B and C both want "they have done this before".
3. **Does anyone win permanently?** Right now there is no health, no rounds and
   no ending. The premise you pick decides whether the game needs one.
4. **Veiled Shadow's name.** It is a description, not a name. Does anyone in
   the fiction call her that, or is it what people call her because they do not
   have one?
