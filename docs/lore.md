# Lore — options, not a bible

Item 5. A fork in the road with three roads on it. Pick one and the rest
becomes writable.

## The sheet already wrote most of this

Everything here is built on the concept sheet's own copy, because the sheet is
not reference art — it is a specification, and it already contains the lore:

> **THE VEILED SHADOW** — assassin, chained blade, 2.00 m
> *"A shadow in reverence. She moves like a whisper in the wind, strikes like
> divine retribution. Her faith is her veil. Her weapon, judgment."*

> **THE PIOUS FORCE** — imam, brawler, 2.80 m
> *"He is the wall between the weak and the wicked. His fists are sermons. His
> scars, verses. He does not seek forgiveness. He delivers it."*

Four things follow that nothing below is allowed to contradict:

1. **They share a faith.** This is not belief against unbelief, and there is no
   version of this game where one of them is the infidel. Her faith is her
   veil; his fists are sermons. Both are devout and both mean it.
2. **The argument is mercy against judgment.** He is a *wall* — he stands
   between, he absorbs, he ends things by being immovable. She is a *blade on a
   chain* — she reaches, she chooses, she ends things by deciding. That is a
   real disagreement between two believers and it needs no villain.
3. **She is armed and he is not.** Chained blade against bare wrapped fists.
   That is in the sheet and the movesets should own it rather than quietly
   turning her into a kickboxer.
4. **0.8 m of height between them.** 2.80 against 2.00 is not a stat line, it
   is the silhouette of every screenshot. He is a head and shoulders of wall.

## What the build already asserts

- **A long stone hall.** Candles down both walls, an apse, a rose window,
  volumetric fog. Two fighters spawn at the near end and walk toward the light.
- **No third party.** No crowd, no announcer, nobody else in the room.

The hall should probably not be a working place of worship. A duel on a
consecrated floor makes the fight an act of desecration and drags the game into
an argument nobody asked for. Every option below assumes it stopped being used
for what it was built for a long time ago.

## Three premises

### A — The Vigil

The hall is a waystation on a road nobody finishes. Whoever holds it keeps the
candles lit. He has kept them for years, standing between the road and whatever
uses it. She arrives when they burn low, and neither of them is sure whether
she is a test or a verdict.

Duels are a rite here, so both can be entirely sincere and still swing at full
force. Best atmosphere match — the candles and the fog are already this.

### B — The Ledger

Something was taken out of the hall long enough ago that nobody agrees what.
Everyone who walks in is claiming a debt, and the fight is not about the debt,
it is about **method** — which is the sheet's own argument made concrete.

He settles in the open, by hand, and considers it closed the moment it is
settled: he does not seek forgiveness, he delivers it. She settles quietly and
never announces it, because a debt collected loudly makes a second one. Two
people who agree the debt is real and agree on nothing after that.

Generates dialogue for free and supports a rematch structure indefinitely.

### C — The Last Room

Outside is gone. Not explained, not shown — gone. The hall is what is left and
these two are the last who remember what it was for. They fight because it is
the only thing left that takes a whole person's attention, and here a person
who stops paying attention stops.

Melancholy, weight on every hit, hardest to write. Best justifies an empty hall.

**Recommendation: B run inside A's ritual.** A gives the room its tone, B gives
the two of them something to say to each other that never resolves.

## The two, under any premise

**Pious Force.** Devout and written that way straight — his faith is where he
thinks his strength comes from and he says so. The one hard rule is the line at
the religion's expense: that is the Venator problem, and it stays out of his
mouth. He does not quote scripture either, in a taunt or anywhere, because holy
text used as trash talk is the same move dressed up as respect. He talks about
himself — what is lent to him, what he does not own, what he will not take
credit for. Recorded: *"I only praise Him."*, *"Strength is lent. Never
owned."*, *"It was never mine to keep."*, and the sheet's own line, *"I do not
seek forgiveness. I deliver it."*

**Veiled Shadow.** The same reverence, pointed the other way. Not a professional
and not a cynic — "a shadow in reverence" is the sheet's phrase and it is the
whole character. She is quiet because quiet is the discipline, and what she says
is about restraint, not about being unseen for its own sake. Recorded:
*"Judgment is not loud."*, *"The veil is not to hide. It is to remember."*,
*"Mercy is his. Silence is mine."*, *"No one heard. Let that be the mercy."*

The same rule that governs him governs her, and one more: the violence is hers,
never delegated upward. She claims judgment as her own choice. Writing her as
carrying out an instruction from God turns a character into an accusation.

## Open, for you

1. **Which premise** — A, B, C, or B inside A.
2. **Do they know each other?** First meeting reads very differently from the
   fortieth; B and C both want "they have done this before".
3. **Does anyone win permanently?** There is no health, no rounds and no ending
   yet. The premise decides whether the game needs one.
4. **Her weapon in the moveset.** The sheet gives her a chained blade and the
   current striker moveset ignores it. Reach is her whole identity against a
   2.80 m wall — worth building before more lore.
